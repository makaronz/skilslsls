## Lighting Fundamentals

Lighting is one of the most powerful tools in video lighting, lighting design, and illumination techniques for film and video, directly affecting mood, clarity, dimension, and visual quality. Understanding light behavior enables precise control over the visual result.

### Light Properties

| Property | Range | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Intensity | Low to high | Exposure, mood, attention |
| Color Temperature | 2700K (warm) to 6500K (cool) | Mood, time of day, realism |
| Quality | Hard to soft | Contrast, shadow definition |
| Direction | Front, side, back, top, bottom | Shape, dimension, drama |
| Spread | Narrow to wide | Focus, coverage area |

## Three-Point Lighting System

The foundation of most lighting setups:

### Key Light
- Primary light source, establishes the dominant lighting direction
- Typically placed 30-45 degrees to one side of the camera and slightly above subject
- Determines the overall exposure level and shadow pattern

### Fill Light
- Reduces shadows created by the key light
- Placed opposite the key light at a lower intensity
- Controls the contrast ratio (key-to-fill ratio)

### Back Light (Rim/Hair Light)
- Separates the subject from the background
- Placed behind and above the subject
- Creates a rim of light around edges for depth and dimension

### Lighting Ratios

| Ratio (Key:Fill) | Look | Application |
|---|---|---|
| 1:1 | Flat, even, broadcast | Corporate, news, beauty |
| 2:1 | Subtle dimension | Interviews, presentations |
| 4:1 | Dramatic, cinematic | Narrative, mood pieces |
| 8:1+ | High contrast, noir | Dramatic, artistic |

## Modifier Selection

| Modifier | Effect | Best For |
|---|---|---|
| Softbox | Soft, even, wrapping light | Interviews, portraits |
| Umbrella | Broad, soft fill | General purpose, events |
| Fresnel | Controllable, hard/soft | Cinematic, theatrical |
| LED Panel | Even, adjustable | Corporate, run-and-gun |
| Bounce/Reflector | Redirected natural or artificial light | Fill, outdoor |
| Flag/Cutter | Blocks and shapes light | Controlling spill |

## Practical Tips

- **Meter and monitor** — Use light meters and waveform monitors for accurate exposure
- **Control spill** — Use flags, barn doors, and grids to prevent unwanted light
- **Match color temperatures** — Ensure all sources are balanced unless mixing intentionally
- **Build incrementally** — Start with one light and add sources one at a time
- **Safety first** — Secure all fixtures, manage cables, and never exceed power ratings
