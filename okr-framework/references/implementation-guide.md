# OKR Implementation Guide

Step-by-step guide for deploying OKRs (Objectives and Key Results) across an organization, from pilot to full rollout.

---

## Implementation Phases

### Phase 1: Education and Alignment (Weeks 1-2)

Train leadership on OKR principles before rolling out to the organization:

| Topic | Key Points | Duration |
|-------|-----------|----------|
| OKR Philosophy | Aspirational goals, transparency, focus, alignment | 1 hour |
| Writing Good OKRs | Objective = qualitative direction, KR = quantitative measure | 2 hours |
| Common Mistakes | Output vs. outcome KRs, too many OKRs, sandbagging | 1 hour |
| OKR Cadence | Quarterly setting, weekly check-ins, quarterly scoring | 30 min |
| Tool Selection | Spreadsheet, Lattice, Ally.io, Weekdone, or custom | 30 min |

### Phase 2: Pilot (Weeks 3-6)

Start with 2-3 teams, not the entire organization:

1. **Select pilot teams**: Choose teams with engaged leadership and clear measurable goals
2. **Set company OKRs first**: Leadership defines 3-5 company-level OKRs for the quarter
3. **Cascade to pilot teams**: Each team sets 2-3 OKRs aligned with company objectives
4. **Weekly check-ins**: 15-minute standing meetings to review KR progress
5. **Mid-quarter review**: Formal check-in at week 6 to assess progress, adjust if needed
6. **End-of-quarter scoring**: Score each KR on a 0.0-1.0 scale

### Phase 3: Full Rollout (Weeks 7-12)

Expand to the entire organization:

1. Share pilot learnings at an all-hands meeting
2. Distribute OKR templates and writing guides
3. Assign OKR champions in each department (peer coaches, not managers)
4. Run OKR-setting workshops for all teams
5. Establish the quarterly cadence

## Writing Effective Objectives

### Objective Checklist

- [ ] Qualitative, not a number (numbers belong in Key Results)
- [ ] Inspiring and motivational — would the team rally around it?
- [ ] Achievable in one quarter (or clearly marked as annual)
- [ ] Clear enough that anyone in the company can understand it
- [ ] Starts with a verb: "Build...", "Establish...", "Transform...", "Deliver..."

### Good vs. Bad Objectives

| Bad | Why | Good |
|-----|-----|------|
| "Increase revenue by 20%" | That is a Key Result, not an Objective | "Become the market leader in mid-market SaaS" |
| "Improve customer satisfaction" | Too vague | "Deliver a world-class onboarding experience" |
| "Launch feature X" | Output, not outcome | "Enable customers to self-serve their reporting needs" |

## Writing Effective Key Results

### Key Result Formula

```
[Verb] [metric] from [current baseline] to [target]
```

Examples:
- Increase NPS from 32 to 50
- Reduce average onboarding time from 14 days to 5 days
- Grow monthly active users from 10,000 to 25,000

### KR Quality Checklist

- [ ] Quantitative and measurable (a number, percentage, or yes/no milestone)
- [ ] Has a clear baseline (current state) and target (desired end state)
- [ ] Outcome-based, not activity-based ("Increase retention to 90%" not "Send 10 retention emails")
- [ ] Stretch but achievable — 70% completion should feel like success
- [ ] Within the team's sphere of influence

## Cadence and Ceremonies

| Ceremony | Frequency | Duration | Purpose |
|----------|-----------|----------|---------|
| OKR Setting | Quarterly | Half-day workshop | Set company and team OKRs for the quarter |
| Weekly Check-in | Weekly | 15 minutes | Update KR progress, flag blockers |
| Mid-Quarter Review | Once (Week 6) | 1 hour | Assess trajectory, adjust approach |
| End-of-Quarter Scoring | Quarterly | 1 hour per team | Score KRs, reflect on learning |
| Annual Planning | Annually | 1-2 day offsite | Set annual Objectives and themes |

## Common Implementation Failures

| Failure | Symptom | Prevention |
|---------|---------|-----------|
| Too many OKRs | Teams have 5+ Objectives with 5+ KRs each | Limit to 3-5 Objectives, 2-4 KRs each |
| No weekly check-ins | OKRs are set and forgotten until end of quarter | Make check-ins a non-negotiable calendar event |
| Using OKRs for performance review | Teams sandbag to ensure 100% completion | Separate OKR scoring from compensation decisions |
| Top-down only | Teams have no ownership, OKRs feel imposed | Allow 40-60% of OKRs to be bottom-up from teams |
| No baseline data | KRs like "improve NPS" with no current measurement | Require baseline measurement before setting the KR |
