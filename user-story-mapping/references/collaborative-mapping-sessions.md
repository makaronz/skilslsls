# Collaborative Mapping Sessions

Facilitating effective user story mapping workshops, including preparation, facilitation techniques, and remote adaptation.

---

## Workshop Preparation

### Before the Session

| Task | Owner | Timeline |
|------|-------|----------|
| Define the scope | PM / Facilitator | 1 week before |
| Identify personas | PM | 1 week before |
| Invite participants (5-8 people) | Facilitator | 1 week before |
| Prepare materials (sticky notes, markers, or digital board) | Facilitator | 1-2 days before |
| Share pre-read (persona profiles, problem statement) | PM | 2-3 days before |
| Set up the room or digital workspace | Facilitator | Day before |

### Participant Selection

| Role | Why They're Needed | Count |
|------|-------------------|-------|
| Product Manager | Owns the vision and priorities | 1 |
| UX Designer | Represents user experience perspective | 1-2 |
| Engineers | Provide feasibility and complexity input | 2-3 |
| Domain Expert | Has deep knowledge of the user's world | 1 |
| Stakeholder | Provides business context and constraints | 1 |

Keep the group to 5-8 people. Larger groups slow consensus and reduce individual engagement.

## Facilitation Techniques

### Building the Backbone

1. **Silent brainstorming** (5 min): Each participant writes activities on sticky notes independently
2. **Share and group** (10 min): Participants place their notes on the board, cluster duplicates
3. **Sequence** (10 min): Collaboratively arrange activities left to right in user journey order
4. **Add tasks** (15 min): Under each activity, add the specific steps the user takes
5. **Narrative walk** (5 min): One person narrates the backbone as a story to validate completeness

### Populating the Body

- Use time-boxed rounds: 10 minutes of silent writing, then 5 minutes of placing and discussing
- Encourage everyone to write stories, not just the PM
- Color-code stories by type (blue = user story, yellow = technical task, pink = question/unknown)
- Place unknowns and questions as explicit cards — they represent research needed

### Prioritization Techniques

| Technique | How It Works | When to Use |
|-----------|-------------|-------------|
| Dot voting | Each participant gets 5 dots to place on most important stories | Quick, democratic ranking |
| Top-down ordering | Facilitator asks "What is the single most important story under this task?" and works down | Structured, thorough |
| MoSCoW on the map | Color-code stories as Must/Should/Could/Won't | Release planning focus |
| Fist of five | Participants show 1-5 fingers for agreement level on priority placement | Consensus check |

### Handling Common Situations

| Situation | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| One person dominates | Use silent brainstorming, round-robin sharing, and explicit turn-taking |
| Group gets stuck on details | Time-box the discussion (2 min max per story), park details in a "parking lot" |
| Disagreement on priority | Use dot voting or fist-of-five to gauge group opinion; PM makes final call |
| Scope keeps expanding | Revisit the session scope statement; add out-of-scope items to a future map |
| Energy drops | Take a break, switch activities, or do a quick energizer exercise |

## Remote Adaptation

### Digital Tools

| Tool | Strengths | Limitations |
|------|-----------|------------|
| Miro | Purpose-built templates, real-time collaboration, voting | Can be slow with large boards |
| FigJam | Integrated with Figma, intuitive, good for design teams | Fewer specialized templates |
| Avion | Dedicated story mapping tool with backlog integration | Smaller user base |
| Notion | Flexible, database-backed cards | No spatial layout native support |
| Google Jamboard | Free, simple | Limited features, being deprecated |

### Remote Session Tips

1. **Smaller groups**: Cap at 6 people for remote sessions (less than in-person)
2. **Cameras on**: Visual cues reduce misunderstanding
3. **Breakout rooms**: For larger groups, split into pairs for story writing, then reconvene
4. **Timer visible**: Use a shared timer for all time-boxed activities
5. **Facilitator actively manages**: Call on people by name, use the chat for parallel input
6. **Record the session**: Capture the evolving board and key discussion points
7. **Pre-populate the backbone**: Start with a draft backbone so remote participants can react rather than create from scratch

### Asynchronous Mapping

For distributed teams across time zones:
1. PM creates initial backbone and shares for async review (24 hours)
2. Team members add stories independently within their time zone
3. Facilitator consolidates, identifies conflicts and gaps
4. One synchronous session (90 min max) to resolve conflicts, prioritize, and draw release lines
5. PM documents the final map and shares for async validation
