# Foley Sound Design Fundamentals

Core concepts, techniques, and workflows for creating and recording Foley sound effects for film, television, and interactive media.

---

## What Is Foley

Foley is the art of creating and recording everyday sound effects in synchronization with picture. Named after sound artist Jack Foley, the discipline replaces or enhances production audio with clean, controllable sounds performed on a Foley stage.

### Foley vs. Sound Effects

| Aspect | Foley | Sound Effects (SFX) |
|--------|-------|-------------------|
| Performance | Performed live to picture | Pre-recorded or synthesized |
| Sync | Frame-accurate to on-screen action | Spotted and edited to picture |
| Content | Everyday human sounds (footsteps, cloth, props) | Environmental, mechanical, fantastical sounds |
| Purpose | Ground the character in the scene | Build the world around the character |

## The Three Categories of Foley

### 1. Footsteps

Footsteps are the most fundamental Foley element. They connect the character to the environment and convey weight, pace, and emotion.

Key variables:
- **Surface**: Wood, concrete, gravel, carpet, metal, water, snow — each requires a different material on the stage
- **Shoe type**: Heels, boots, sneakers, bare feet — match the on-screen character
- **Gait**: Walk, run, shuffle, limp, sneak — match the performance rhythm
- **Weight**: Light character vs. heavy character — adjust pressure and technique

### 2. Cloth

Cloth movement (also called "moves") provides the subtle sound of clothing as characters gesture, sit, stand, and walk. Without cloth Foley, characters feel disconnected from their bodies.

Techniques:
- Use fabrics matching the on-screen costume (denim, silk, leather, cotton)
- Perform arm swings, torso twists, and sitting motions in sync with picture
- Layer cloth under footsteps for a natural, full-body sound

### 3. Props

Props cover everything a character touches, picks up, sets down, or interacts with:
- Doors (open, close, lock, knock)
- Dishes (clink, set down, wash)
- Weapons (draw, holster, reload)
- Paper (turn page, crumple, write)
- Electronics (type, phone pick up, button press)

## Recording Setup

### Foley Stage Requirements

| Element | Specification | Purpose |
|---------|--------------|---------|
| Room | Acoustically dead (no reverb) | Clean recordings; reverb added in mix |
| Floor pits | Removable sections with different surfaces | Quick surface changes for footsteps |
| Microphone | Large diaphragm condenser (e.g., Neumann U87) | Detail and warmth |
| Mic placement | 2-4 feet from the sound source | Natural perspective without room coloration |
| Monitoring | Video playback with timecode sync | Perform in sync with picture |
| Preamp | Low-noise, high-gain capable | Capture quiet details cleanly |

### Recording Levels

- Target peak level: -12 dBFS to -6 dBFS
- Leave headroom for transient spikes (impacts, slaps)
- Record at 48 kHz / 24-bit minimum (96 kHz for detail-critical work)

## Performance Technique

### Syncing to Picture

1. Watch the scene 2-3 times to internalize the rhythm
2. Perform a practice pass without recording
3. Record in short sections (5-15 seconds) rather than attempting the full scene
4. Use three-beep pre-roll for consistent sync reference
5. Record multiple takes — give the editor options

### Selling the Performance

Foley is acting with sound. The performance must convey:
- **Character weight and physicality**: A large character sounds different from a small one
- **Emotional state**: Angry footsteps are heavier and faster; nervous footsteps are lighter and irregular
- **Environment**: Sounds in a bathroom have different energy than sounds in an open field (the reverb will be added in post, but the performance energy should match)

## Post-Production Workflow

1. **Spotting session**: Watch the project and identify all Foley cues needed
2. **Recording session**: Perform and record all cues in category order (all footsteps, then cloth, then props)
3. **Editing**: Trim, align, and crossfade takes for seamless sync
4. **Premix**: EQ, compression, and reverb to blend Foley with dialogue and SFX
5. **Final mix**: Balance Foley levels within the full sound mix
