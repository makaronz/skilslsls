# Technical Preparation

Interview techniques, preparation strategies, and question frameworks for documentary filmmaking, non-fiction storytelling, and real-world film production.

---

## Interview Planning

Effective interviews in documentary filmmaking, non-fiction storytelling, and real-world film production require thorough preparation, skilled execution, and careful follow-up to capture valuable insights and content.

### Pre-Interview Preparation

| Task | Details | Timeline |
|---|---|---|
| Research | Study the subject, topic, and context thoroughly | 1-2 weeks before |
| Question Development | Draft questions aligned with objectives | 1 week before |
| Logistics | Confirm location, equipment, scheduling | 3-5 days before |
| Technical Setup | Test all recording equipment and backups | Day before |
| Pre-Interview Brief | Discuss process and expectations with the interviewee | Day before or day of |

---

## Question Design

### Question Types

- **Open-ended questions** — Encourage detailed, narrative responses ("Tell me about..." "Describe your experience with...")
- **Specific follow-ups** — Probe deeper into interesting responses ("Can you give me a specific example?" "What happened next?")
- **Comparative questions** — Reveal perspective and values ("How does this compare to..." "What was different about...")
- **Reflective questions** — Prompt analysis and insight ("Looking back, what would you..." "What did you learn from...")

### Question Sequence Strategy

1. **Warm-up** — Easy, comfortable questions to build rapport
2. **Core topics** — Primary questions addressing the main objectives
3. **Deep exploration** — Follow-up questions on the most promising responses
4. **Challenging questions** — More difficult or sensitive topics once trust is established
5. **Closing** — Summary, final thoughts, anything the interviewee wants to add

## Interview Techniques

### Building Rapport

- Begin with genuine interest and active listening
- Use the interviewee's name and reference their background
- Show engagement through body language and verbal acknowledgments
- Create a comfortable, non-judgmental environment

### Active Listening

- Focus completely on the speaker without planning your next question
- Note emotional cues and energy shifts for follow-up
- Allow natural pauses — silence often prompts deeper responses
- Summarize and reflect back to confirm understanding

### Managing the Conversation

- Guide gently without controlling — follow interesting threads
- Redirect tactfully when the conversation goes off-topic
- Be prepared to abandon prepared questions when better topics emerge
- Watch the clock while maintaining natural flow

---

## Post-Interview Process

1. **Review notes immediately** — Capture fresh impressions and insights
2. **Organize material** — Categorize responses by theme and relevance
3. **Identify key moments** — Flag the most compelling or useful content
4. **Follow up** — Thank the interviewee and clarify any ambiguities
5. **Integrate** — Incorporate interview content into the larger project with proper context
