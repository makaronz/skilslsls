# Shot Lists And Ideas

Camera operation techniques, shot design principles, and filming strategies for documentary filmmaking, non-fiction storytelling, and real-world film production.

---

## Camera Fundamentals

Effective camera work in documentary filmmaking, non-fiction storytelling, and real-world film production combines technical knowledge with creative vision to capture compelling visual content that serves the story or message.

### Exposure Triangle

| Parameter | Effect | Adjustment |
|---|---|---|
| Aperture (f-stop) | Depth of field, light intake | Lower = shallower DOF, more light |
| Shutter Speed | Motion blur, sharpness | Faster = sharper motion, less light |
| ISO | Sensor sensitivity, noise | Higher = more sensitive, more noise |

**Rule of thumb**: For standard video, set shutter speed to double the frame rate (e.g., 1/50 for 24fps), then adjust aperture and ISO for proper exposure.

## Shot Composition

### Essential Shot Types

| Shot | Frame | Purpose | When to Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wide/Establishing | Full environment | Set context, show scale | Scene openings, transitions |
| Medium | Subject waist-up | Show action and interaction | Dialogue, demonstrations |
| Close-Up | Face or detail | Show emotion, emphasize | Reactions, important details |
| Extreme Close-Up | Single detail | Dramatic emphasis | Key moments, details |
| Over-the-Shoulder | Behind one subject | Create perspective | Conversations, POV |

### Composition Principles

- **Rule of Thirds** — Place key elements at intersection points of a 3x3 grid
- **Leading Lines** — Use lines in the frame to guide the viewer's eye
- **Headroom** — Leave appropriate space above subjects' heads
- **Look Room** — Leave space in the direction a subject is looking or moving
- **Depth** — Create foreground, midground, and background layers for visual interest

## Camera Movement

### Movement Types and Applications

- **Static** — Locked tripod shot for stability and formal framing
- **Pan** — Horizontal rotation to follow action or reveal environment
- **Tilt** — Vertical rotation to show scale or follow vertical action
- **Dolly/Track** — Camera moves through space for immersive following shots
- **Handheld** — Natural, organic movement for documentary or intimate feel
- **Gimbal/Stabilizer** — Smooth movement with freedom of handheld

### Movement Best Practices

1. Every camera movement should have a purpose — avoid unmotivated movement
2. Start and end movements on well-composed frames
3. Match movement speed to the emotional tone of the scene
4. Practice movements before recording to ensure smooth execution
5. Provide sufficient static coverage in addition to moving shots
