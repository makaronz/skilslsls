# Wildlife Field Craft and Ethics

Approach and photograph wildlife responsibly with minimal disturbance.

---

## Ethical Guidelines

### Core Principles

1. **Animal welfare comes first**: Never endanger, harass, or stress wildlife
2. **Respect habitat**: Stay on trails, don't damage vegetation or nests
3. **Legal compliance**: Follow local regulations, permit requirements
4. **Honest representation**: No baiting, luring, or captive animals presented as wild (disclose if used)
5. **Share knowledge responsibly**: Don't publish locations of sensitive species

### Signs of Disturbance

| Behavior | Species | Action Required |
|----------|---------|----------------|
| Alarm calls | Birds | Immediately back away |
| Flight/running away | All | Stop approach, increase distance |
| Staring directly at you | Mammals | Freeze, avoid eye contact |
| Nest departure | Birds | Retreat completely, don't return |
| Changed behavior | All | You're too close |
| Defensive posture | Mammals, reptiles | Slowly back away |

### Minimum Approach Distances

| Species | Minimum Distance | Notes |
|---------|-----------------|-------|
| Nesting birds | 50-100m+ | Species-dependent, follow local laws |
| Large mammals (bears, moose) | 100m / 300ft | Never between mother and offspring |
| Marine mammals | 50-100m | Regulations vary by country |
| Raptors at nest | 200m+ | Extremely sensitive to disturbance |
| General songbirds | 10-30m | Use telephoto, minimize time |

## Field Craft Techniques

### Approaching Wildlife

| Method | Description | Best For |
|--------|-------------|----------|
| Stalking | Slow, deliberate approach | Open country, large mammals |
| Hide/blind | Concealed stationary position | Nesting birds, water holes |
| Vehicle approach | Using vehicle as mobile blind | Safari, roadside wildlife |
| Float approach | Kayak or canoe | Waterbirds, shore wildlife |
| Wait and watch | Patient observation at known spot | Predictable paths/behavior |

### Stalking Principles
- Move slowly during calm behavior; freeze during alert behavior
- Keep low — smaller silhouette is less threatening
- Use natural cover (trees, rocks, terrain)
- Approach at an angle, never directly toward the animal
- Avoid sudden movements
- If the animal looks at you, stop and wait
- Wind direction: stay downwind (scent away from animal)

### Blind/Hide Setup
- Set up well before dawn or while subject is away
- Use natural materials to camouflage
- Leave enough time for wildlife to acclimate (ideally days)
- Enter and exit when subject is not present
- Have entrance away from subject direction
- Portable hides: pop-up fabric blinds with camo pattern

## Seasonal and Timing Strategies

### Best Times to Photograph

| Time | Activity | Quality |
|------|----------|---------|
| Dawn (golden hour) | Peak activity, feeding | Best light, active subjects |
| Early morning (1-3h after dawn) | Continued activity | Good light, warming |
| Midday | Rest period for most species | Harsh light, limited activity |
| Late afternoon (2-3h before sunset) | Renewed activity | Improving light |
| Dusk (golden hour) | Feeding, returning to roost | Best light, active subjects |
| Night | Nocturnal species | Requires specialized equipment |

### Seasonal Considerations
- **Spring**: Breeding behavior, nesting, bright plumage, migration arrival
- **Summer**: Raising young, fledglings, territorial behavior
- **Autumn**: Migration, rut (mammals), color change
- **Winter**: Easier to spot (bare trees), snow backgrounds, concentrated feeding

## Safety

### Personal Safety
- Inform others of your location and expected return
- Carry emergency supplies (water, first aid, communication device)
- Know dangerous species in the area
- Never approach or corner a wild animal
- Be aware of weather changes
- Wear appropriate clothing (neutral colors, layers)
- Watch for ticks, snakes, and environmental hazards

### Equipment Safety
- Rain covers for gear in wet conditions
- Secure tripods on uneven terrain
- Use lens caps in transit to prevent damage
- Protect gear from sand, salt spray, extreme temperatures
