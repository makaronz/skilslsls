# Animal Behavior for Wildlife Photography

Understand and anticipate animal behavior to capture compelling wildlife moments.

---

## Behavioral Cues

### Reading Body Language

| Cue | Meaning | Photo Opportunity |
|-----|---------|------------------|
| Head turning, scanning | Alert, watching for danger | Vigilant portrait |
| Stretching, yawning | Relaxed, waking up | Intimate moment |
| Ruffling feathers | Maintenance, comfort | Detail shots |
| Crouching low | Preparing to pounce/flee | Action anticipation |
| Tail wagging/flicking | Agitation or communication | Behavioral story |
| Wing spreading | Drying, thermoregulation, display | Dramatic pose |
| Ear positioning | Attention direction (mammals) | Anticipate head turn |
| Tongue flicking (reptiles) | Sensing environment | Character portrait |

### Pre-Action Indicators

Anticipate action by watching for these signals:

| Animal | Pre-Flight Signal | Lead Time |
|--------|------------------|-----------|
| Birds (takeoff) | Crouch, defecate, lean forward | 0.5-2 seconds |
| Raptors (dive) | Head lock-on, body tense | 1-3 seconds |
| Mammals (run) | Ear perk, head lift, freeze | 1-5 seconds |
| Fish (breach) | Surface disturbance | 0-1 second |
| Insects (flight) | Wing vibration, leg adjustment | 0.1-0.5 seconds |

## Species-Specific Behavior

### Birds

**Daily Patterns:**
- Dawn: Chorus, territorial singing, initial feeding
- Morning: Active feeding, social behavior
- Midday: Rest, preening, bathing
- Afternoon: Renewed feeding, flock activity
- Dusk: Roosting flight, evening chorus

**Key Behaviors to Photograph:**
| Behavior | Timing | Setup |
|----------|--------|-------|
| Feeding | Morning/evening | Near food source, fast shutter |
| Courtship display | Breeding season | Hide at display ground, patience |
| Nest building | Spring | Distant observation, telephoto |
| Fledging | Late spring/summer | Near nest (carefully), continuous burst |
| Bathing | Mid-morning | Near shallow water |
| Territorial fights | Breeding season | Known territory boundaries |

### Mammals

**Predator Behavior Cycle:**
1. Rest/sleep (60-70% of time)
2. Alerting/scanning
3. Stalking/pursuing
4. Kill/feeding
5. Social interaction
6. Return to rest

**Best Photo Opportunities:**
- Transition from rest to alert
- Hunting sequences
- Parenting behavior (carrying, nursing, playing)
- Territorial displays
- Water crossings (wildebeest, caribou)
- Sunrise/sunset activity periods

## Habitat and Location Scouting

### Finding Subjects

| Method | Effectiveness | Effort |
|--------|-------------|--------|
| Known hotspots (eBird, iNaturalist) | High | Low |
| Local guide/expert | Very high | Medium (cost) |
| Habitat analysis | Medium | High |
| Trail cameras | High | Medium |
| Sound identification | High for birds | Medium |
| Track/sign reading | Medium | High |

### Habitat Associations

| Habitat | Common Subjects | Best Season |
|---------|----------------|-------------|
| Wetlands | Wading birds, ducks, amphibians | Spring-autumn |
| Forests | Songbirds, deer, squirrels | Year-round |
| Grasslands | Raptors, ground-nesting birds | Spring-summer |
| Coastal | Seabirds, marine mammals | Migration season |
| Mountains | Alpine species, raptors | Summer-autumn |
| Urban parks | Songbirds, waterfowl | Year-round |

## Patience and Persistence

### Wildlife Photography Mindset
- **Expect failure**: Most sessions don't produce exceptional images
- **Return to locations**: Familiarity reveals patterns
- **Study subjects beforehand**: Research behavior before fieldwork
- **Be prepared for unexpected**: Keep camera settings ready
- **Document everything**: Notes on behavior, conditions, timing
- **Learn from misses**: Analyze what went wrong, adjust approach
- **Respect the process**: Ethical, patient photography produces better results
