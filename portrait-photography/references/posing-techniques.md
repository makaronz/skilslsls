# Portrait Posing Techniques

Direct subjects into flattering, natural-looking poses for professional portraits.

---

## Foundation Poses

### Standing Poses

| Pose | Direction | Effect |
|------|-----------|--------|
| Weight on back foot | Shift weight to rear leg | Slimming, relaxed |
| Angled body (45°) | Turn body 45° from camera | Slimming, dynamic |
| S-curve | Shift hip opposite to shoulder lean | Feminine, elegant |
| Hands in pockets (partial) | Thumbs out or fingers showing | Relaxed, masculine |
| Arms crossed | Low fold, not tight | Confident, approachable |
| Hand on hip | One hand, elbow back | Confident, defines waist |

### Seated Poses

| Pose | Setup | Effect |
|------|-------|--------|
| Lean forward | Elbows on knees, back straight | Engaged, confident |
| Cross-legged | Ankle over knee | Relaxed, casual |
| Turned on chair | Straddle backwards | Creative, casual |
| Edge of seat | Perched forward | Alert, dynamic |

## Body Parts Guide

### Hands
- Most common posing problem
- Give hands something to do: hold object, touch face, adjust clothing
- Avoid flat palms toward camera (appear large)
- Show edge of hand when possible
- Fingers should be slightly separated, not clenched
- Gentle touch, never pressing (creates white knuckles)

### Chin and Jawline
- Extend chin slightly forward and down ("turtle" move)
- Defines jawline and reduces double chin
- Subtle — overdoing it looks unnatural
- Works for virtually every face shape

### Shoulders
- Drop shoulders down (people tense when photographed)
- Angle one shoulder toward camera (avoid square-on)
- Create asymmetry between shoulder heights
- Slight lean creates more dynamic feel

### Eyes
- Have subject look at a specific spot (not vaguely "over there")
- Catch light should be visible in eyes
- Squinch: slight squint of lower eyelid = more confident look
- Direct eye contact: powerful, engaging
- Looking away: contemplative, editorial

## Posing by Subject Type

### Business/Corporate Headshots
- Shoulders squared or slight angle
- Confident expression, slight smile
- Hands: crossed arms (not tight) or at sides
- Chin forward and down for jawline
- Eye-level camera angle

### Couples
- Create closeness: touching foreheads, hands, intertwined
- Vary heights: one seated, one standing
- Create triangles between faces
- Direct interaction: walking, laughing, looking at each other
- One looking at camera, one looking at partner

### Groups
- Vary head heights (sitting, standing, leaning)
- Create triangles and diagonals
- Minimize gaps between people
- Shoot slightly above for group flattery
- Odd numbers in subgroups

### Children
- Get on their level (camera at eye height)
- Let them move and play; capture in between
- Use parents or helpers behind camera
- Gentle direction, not rigid posing
- Capture genuine expressions and interaction

## Coaching Communication

### Verbal Cues
- "Take a deep breath and drop your shoulders" (relaxation)
- "Lean slightly toward me" (engagement)
- "Turn your body this way, face back to me" (angle)
- "Think about [happy memory]" (genuine expression)
- "Now give me a slow, slight smile" (controlled expression)
- "Shake it out!" (reset between poses)

### Demonstration
- Show the pose yourself first
- Mirror the subject's movements
- Use gentle physical cues (point, gesture — never touch without permission)
- Start with easy, comfortable poses and build to complex

### Energy Management
- Start with simple, confidence-building poses
- Increase complexity gradually
- Give positive feedback frequently
- Take breaks for wardrobe/makeup adjustments
- End with favorite poses while energy is still high
